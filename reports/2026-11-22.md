# Elicit — AI Scientific Research Assistant

- **Date checked:** 2026-11-22, 16:58 PKT
- **Scheduled category:** General-purpose, research, reasoning and multimodal AI
- **Developer:** Elicit Research, PBC
- **Verdict:** A useful free starting point for academic-paper discovery and first-pass evidence synthesis. Its free paper search, summaries and paper chat are generous, but deeper Research Agent and Report work is constrained by an undisclosed monthly usage pool. It must not be used as the only search method for a formal systematic review.

## What it does

Elicit is a specialist AI research assistant. It uses semantic search to find academic papers, summarizes studies, answers questions about papers, extracts structured information and generates cited research reports. The Research Agent can work with the wider web and uploaded research materials as well as Elicit's paper corpus. Official help says one Agent session can use up to 100 uploaded resources, with a combined size of up to 10GB.

Elicit's homepage lists more than 138 million academic papers and 545,000 clinical trials. Those corpus figures are vendor-published. The Basic pricing card specifically advertises paper search; unlimited clinical-trial search is listed on a paid plan, so it is not counted as a free-tier entitlement here.

## Why it is worth trying

Use it when a question needs academic evidence rather than a general chatbot response. The free plan can help create an initial reading list, summarize papers, ask questions about paper contents and inspect the sources behind answers. It can be a practical first pass for background research, comparing study findings or identifying papers worth reading.

It is distinct from a general notebook assistant: Elicit is built around academic-paper discovery and evidence extraction, while a notebook tool primarily organizes a user's supplied source collection.

## Free tier and exact limits

**Basic: $0**

The official pricing page lists:
- Unlimited search across more than 138 million papers.
- Unlimited summaries across papers.
- Unlimited chat with papers with full-text access.
- Ability to view sources for answers.
- Zotero import.

The Research Agent and Research Reports are available on all plans, but their usage is limited by a shared monthly pool. The official usage article says consumption depends on the size and complexity of the task; it does not publish a numeric Basic-plan allowance. The pool resets monthly and unused usage does not roll over. When the allowance is exhausted, the Research Agent remains available for Find Papers use cases until the next reset.

The full Systematic Review workflow is available only on Pro, Scale and Enterprise plans.

## Main limitations and cautions

1. **Unpublished free-agent quota:** Elicit does not state a fixed number of free Agent sessions or Reports. A large report or complex analysis may consume more of the pool than a short question.
2. **Search completeness:** Do not treat Elicit as a complete substitute for conventional academic databases. A peer-reviewed 2025 evaluation across four evidence syntheses found average search sensitivity of 39.5% for Elicit versus 94.5% for the original review searches. The authors recommended it as a supplementary search tool, not a replacement.
3. **Full-text access varies:** Elicit's help documentation says that when it cannot access a paper's PDF, extraction may be limited to the abstract.
4. **Android:** No official Elicit Research, PBC Android app was verified. Google Play has a similarly named “Elicit AI Research” app by Yeteamapps, but it is a different study/essay product and is not the Elicit service.
5. **Privacy-document age:** The general Terms and Privacy Policy linked from the site display a last-updated date of 2023. Review the current terms and any applicable organization agreement before uploading confidential, personal or client material.

## Quality and adoption evidence

### Vendor claims

Elicit's homepage says the service is trusted by over 5 million researchers and searches more than 138 million papers and 545,000 clinical trials. These are vendor-reported adoption and corpus figures, not independently audited counts.

### Independent evidence

- A 2025 proof-of-concept study compared Elicit extraction with human extraction across **43 studies and 602 data points**. It reported **81.4% accuracy** for Elicit versus **86.7%** for human reviewers; the difference was not statistically significant.
- A separate 2025 study across **30 articles** reported **92% precision, recall and F1** for Elicit data extraction, but identified four confabulations in the extracted data.
- A 2025 evaluation of four evidence syntheses found average search sensitivity of **39.5%** for Elicit versus **94.5%** for the original review searches. Its conclusion was that Elicit may be a useful supplementary search tool but should not replace traditional search methods.

These studies evaluate different tasks and earlier product versions; they should not be collapsed into one universal accuracy score.

### Anecdotal community feedback

In a May 2026 Reddit comparison of five AI research tools, one user rated Elicit **3/5**, saying it helped organize papers and produce structured insights but felt rigid in places. This is anecdotal feedback from one user, not representative evidence.

**Hands-on testing:** No account was created and no live research task was run for this report. The claims above were checked against public product pages, documentation, research papers and community discussion.
